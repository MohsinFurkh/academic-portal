Two features that surprise people

Examination is on request. An application can sit unexamined until you file the request — and if you miss the deadline, the application is treated as withdrawn.

The clock always runs from the priority date, not from the date you happen to do something. Term, publication and examination deadlines are all measured from it.

Where to file

India has patent offices at Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata; the appropriate office depends on the applicant's address or place of business. Filing is electronic in practice.

Fees are tiered: natural persons, start-ups and small entities pay substantially less than large entities — one reason to file through a university IP cell that knows the categories.

Stage 1 · Filing

What an application actually consists of

The documents

Form 1Application for grant — applicants, inventors, priority details
Form 2Provisional or complete specification, with drawings
Form 3Statement and undertaking about corresponding foreign applications — must be kept updated
Form 5Declaration as to inventorship, filed with the complete specification
Form 26Power of authority, if an agent acts for you
Form 28Claim to small entity or start-up status, with evidence

Form numbers and fees are set by the Patents Rules and are amended from time to time — check the current schedule.

Three decisions taken at filing

1 · Provisional or complete? Provisional buys a date for what it discloses, with twelve months to complete.

2 · Who are the applicants? Usually the institution, per the IP policy. Inventors are named separately and truthfully.

3 · Will you file abroad? If yes, the Paris year and the foreign-filing permission requirement from Part 3 both start mattering now.

Form 3 is the one students' supervisors forget. It is a continuing duty: when you file the corresponding application abroad, you must update the Indian office.

Stage 5 · Prosecution

Three ways to answer an objection — and the cost of each

1 · Argue

Show the cited document does not disclose the feature, or that a skilled person would not have combined the documents. Costs nothing in scope — but only works when the argument is genuinely available.

2 · Amend

Narrow the claim by adding a feature that the prior art lacks. Costs scope permanently — and you may only add what was already disclosed; new matter is not allowed.

3 · Divide or drop

File a divisional for a distinct invention caught by a unity objection, or abandon claims that cannot be saved and keep the ones that can.

Why the Section 3(k) objection is answered differently

For a software invention the objection is usually not “this is old” but “this is not an invention at all”. Narrowing the algorithm does not help. What helps is re-characterising the contribution around its technical effect — the hardware constraint it satisfies, the technical process it improves, the measurable effect outside the program itself — and having written that into the specification at filing time, because you cannot add it later.

Stage 6 · Third parties

India lets the public into the process, twice

Pre-grant opposition

Any person may file a representation after publication and before grant, on grounds such as lack of novelty, obviousness, Section 3 exclusions, insufficiency or wrongful obtaining.

Cheap and widely used — it is how weak applications are stopped before they become rights.

Post-grant opposition

An interested person may oppose within twelve months of grant. It is heard by an Opposition Board and can lead to the patent being revoked or amended.

Revocation

Later, a patent can still be revoked — before the appropriate authority, or as a counter-claim in an infringement suit. Suing someone therefore puts your own patent at risk.

The lesson for an applicant: a granted patent is not the end of the argument. Claims that were stretched during prosecution are exactly what an opponent attacks — which is another reason to keep claim 1 supported by what you actually demonstrated, and to have dependent claims worth falling back on.

Stage 7 · Life after grant

A patent is a subscription, not a purchase

Obligations that keep it alive

  • Renewal fees, payable periodically from the third year onward and rising over the term. Miss them and the patent lapses.
  • Statement of working — India requires patentees to report whether and how the invention is being worked in the country, on the prescribed form and interval.
  • Keeping records of assignments and licences on the register, so that title is clear when someone wants to license it.

Rights you now hold — and their limits

The right to prevent others from making, using, selling, offering for sale or importing the claimed invention in India, for 20 years from the filing date of the application.

Limits: acts done for research or experiment on the invention, use by government in prescribed circumstances, and compulsory licensing — available on stated conditions after three years from grant, where reasonable requirements of the public are not met, the invention is not available at a reasonably affordable price, or it is not worked in India.

The working requirement and compulsory licensing are the state's side of the bargain from Part 1: a monopoly that is never used does not serve the purpose the monopoly was granted for. Unit VI Part 1 takes this up as licensing policy.

The PCT

One application, one search, and thirty months of optionality

What a PCT filing gives you

  • A single international application, with a filing date effective in every contracting state you may later choose
  • An International Search Report and a written opinion on novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability
  • International publication at 18 months from priority
  • Optionally, a demand for international preliminary examination and a second, fuller opinion
  • Deferral of most national costs to 30 or 31 months from priority

What it does not give you

There is no international patent. The search report and opinion are not binding on any national office. Every designated country examines the application under its own law — so a favourable PCT opinion can still meet a Section 3(k) objection in India, or a subject-matter objection in the US.

It also does not extend your total term: the twenty years still run from the international filing date.

Think of the PCT as buying information and time. For a university group with no licensee yet, those are the two scarcest things.

The PCT

Entering the Indian national phase

What happens

A PCT application designating India is filed in the Indian national phase within the prescribed period from priority. It is then treated as an ordinary Indian application: request for examination, FER, response, opposition, grant.

Amendments made during the international phase may be carried in, and the claims must be brought into line with Indian requirements — including Section 3, which no international opinion has considered.

Two practical traps

1 · Section 3 objections arrive late. A favourable international opinion says nothing about Indian excluded subject matter, so a software case can look healthy for two years and then meet 3(k).

2 · Costs land together. National-phase entry in several countries means translations, local agents and fees in the same quarter — which is why the licensing conversation in Unit VI should start well before month 30.

For the running example: the segmentation method would plausibly be filed in India first, taken into the PCT at month 12, and entered in India plus one or two markets where imaging vendors actually operate. Whether anything is granted turns on whether the specification — written at month 0 — framed the contribution as a technical effect rather than as an algorithm.

Try it · 20 minutes

Docket your own application

In your team (12 min)

  1. Take your Part 2 claim. Assume you file a provisional today: write the date.
  2. Build a docket: complete specification, PCT filing, publication, request for examination, expected FER, national-phase entry. Give each a date, not a month number.
  3. Mark the two dates that, if missed, kill the application.
  4. Write the one sentence of technical effect you would put in the specification to survive a Section 3(k) objection.
  5. Name who pays at each stage.

Swap and audit (8 min)

  • Is anything published before the first filing?
  • Are the two fatal dates the right ones — request for examination, and the FER response?
  • Does the technical-effect sentence describe something outside the program itself, or just restate the algorithm?
  • Does the plan account for renewal fees and the working statement after grant?
  • Does it assume a PCT filing grants anything?

Key takeaways

Five things about getting a patent granted

1

Every deadline runs from the priority date — publication, examination, term.

2

Examination happens only on request, and missing the request or the FER response abandons the application.

3

Answering an FER means argue, amend or divide — and a Section 3(k) objection is answered by technical effect written in at filing.

4

India allows pre-grant and post-grant opposition, and a patent can be revoked later — grant is not the end of the argument.

5

The PCT buys time and information, never a right; national offices still decide.
