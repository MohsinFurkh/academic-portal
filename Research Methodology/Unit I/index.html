Syllabus covered in this unit
  • Formulating the problem: meaning and significance of a research problem, its sources, identification and selection, the criteria and characteristics of a good problem, and the errors made in choosing one.
  • Operationalising the problem: defining scope and objectives, selecting an investigative approach, collecting and analysing data, and interpreting results with appropriate instrumentation.

Course outcomes addressed — CO1: understand the meaning, significance and foundational components of research, including the formulation and evaluation of research problems. CO2: utilise investigative approaches and methodologies for problem-solving in computing science research.

How this unit is organised

Seven sets of student notes, one per lecture, written for revision. Each opens as a single reading document with a contents sidebar, a dark/light toggle and a Print / PDF button, and links to the next at the foot of the page.

The unit divides in two. Lectures 1–3 are about choosing a research problem — what one is, how to judge it, and how selection goes wrong. Lectures 4–7 are about executing it — bounding the study, picking an investigative approach, gathering and analysing data, and interpreting what comes back.

3. Reading Path

Read the lecture notes in order — each one builds on the last, and each links to the next at the foot of the page. If you are preparing something specific:

  • Choosing your project topic — Lectures 1, 2 and 3, then write a problem statement and test it against the ATTR-FEAS checklist.
  • Writing the synopsis or proposal — Lecture 4 for scope and SMART objectives, then Lecture 5 to justify your approach.
  • Running the study — Lectures 6 and 7 for collection, analysis, instrumentation and interpretation.
