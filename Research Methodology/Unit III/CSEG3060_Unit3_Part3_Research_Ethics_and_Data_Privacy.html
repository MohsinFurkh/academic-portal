Why ethics comes first

An MRI scan is not just an image. It belongs to a patient.

A diagnosis

A brain tumour is some of the most sensitive health information a person has.

An identity

Files carry names, IDs and dates, and a 3D head scan contains a face.

A trust

Patients were scanned for care, not for your thesis. Reuse needs permission.

A · Honesty in practice

The quiet cheat: data leakage between patients

✗ Split by slice

Train P1P2P1P3P2
Test P1P3

Neighbouring slices of the same patient look almost identical. The model is tested on brains it has already seen, so Dice looks better than it really is.

✓ Split by patient

Train P1P1P2P2
Test P3P3

Every scan of a patient goes to exactly one set. The test score now measures performance on genuinely new patients.

Reporting leaked results as real is an honesty failure. Check your split.

B · Data privacy

Three places a scan hides a patient's identity

1

The file header

DICOM headers store name, patient ID, birth date, scan date and hospital.

Fix: strip or replace every identifying tag

2

The face

A 3D head MRI can be rendered into a face. A 2019 study in NEJM matched such reconstructions to photos with face-recognition software.

Fix: “deface” volumes before sharing

3

The pixels

Some images have names or dates burned into the pixels; a rare condition in a small town can identify someone alone.

Fix: inspect images; limit rare details

Face study: Schwarz et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2019

C · Privacy laws

India's DPDP Act in your terms

Data Principal

The patient whose scan it is.

Data Fiduciary

Whoever decides why and how the data is used: the hospital, the university.

Consent

Free, specific, informed and unambiguous, for a stated purpose.

Purpose limitation

Scans shared for tumour research cannot quietly be reused for something else.

Rights

Access, correction and erasure; consent can be withdrawn.

Children

Under 18 needs verifiable parental consent: relevant for paediatric tumours.

The Act allows some processing for research under conditions and prescribed standards. Your ethics committee, not you, decides how that applies.

D · Ethics review

The IRB checks your plan before any data moves

An Institutional Review Board, called an Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) in India, reviews research involving people or their data. Indian IECs follow the ICMR National Ethical Guidelines (2017).

Approval comes first. Collecting data and asking later is a violation, however good the intentions.

Using old, stored scans? The committee may allow a waiver of consent. The committee decides that, not the researcher.

What the committee reviews

  • Purpose and scientific value
  • Risks to patients, and how they are reduced
  • Consent form, or case for a waiver
  • De-identification and storage plan
  • Who can access the data, and for how long
  • Plans for publishing and sharing

E · The correct procedure

Your checklist: before, during, after

Before collection

  • Written agreement with the hospital
  • IEC approval of the protocol
  • Consent form, or approved waiver
  • Data management plan
  • Parental consent for under-18s

During collection

  • De-identify inside the hospital
  • Deface 3D volumes
  • Store encrypted on approved servers
  • Keep the ID key separate
  • Log who accesses the data

After / publication

  • Share code, not raw scans
  • Report results on BraTS too
  • Release only approved data
  • Report failures and subgroups
  • Delete data as promised

E · Doing it right

Reproducible research without exposing patients

Share the code and settings

Training scripts, pre-processing steps, hyperparameters and random seeds.

Benchmark on public data

Report BraTS results too, so anyone can compare without your private scans.

Use controlled access

Release approved, de-identified data through repositories that require a data-use agreement.

Treat model weights with care

Models can memorise training data. Ask the committee before releasing weights trained on private scans.

Key takeaways

Four rules for research with patient data

1

Honest results start with an honest split: divide by patient.

2

Headers, faces and pixels can all identify a patient.

3

For Indian hospital data, the DPDP Act applies; approval comes before collection.

4

Reproducibility never overrides privacy: share code and benchmarks, not patients.
