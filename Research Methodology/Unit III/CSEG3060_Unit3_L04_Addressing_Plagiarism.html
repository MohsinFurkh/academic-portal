Context

The surface area for misconduct has expanded

Digital repositories, open-access journals, generative AI and code-sharing platforms accelerate innovation — and simultaneously expand the surface area for academic misconduct.

The trend

A 2023 Retraction Watch analysis indicates plagiarism-related retractions in computer science rose approximately 38% over the preceding decade.

Its composition

A significant proportion is attributed to inadequate paraphrasing and improper reuse of prior work — not to brazen copying.

Definition

Four authoritative definitions, and four constituents

From Latin plagiarius — “kidnapper”.

SourceDefinition
ORI (USA)“the appropriation of another person's ideas, processes, results, or words without giving appropriate credit”
Council of Science Editorsusing the work, ideas or words of others without proper attribution, irrespective of intent
IEEE Publication Policy“copying ideas, text, data, software, or any other material without proper citation”
UGC (India) Regulations 2018taking someone else's work or ideas and passing them off as one's own

1 · Originality of source

The material originates from a verifiable source

2 · Lack of attribution

Credit not commensurate with what was borrowed

3 · Intent or negligence

Deliberate deception or reckless disregard

4 · Scope of appropriation

From a phrase to an entire manuscript

Note what ORI and IEEE both name: processes, results, data and software — not merely words. And note the decisive phrase: irrespective of intent. Negligence suffices.

Type 1 of 4

Direct plagiarism: verbatim copying

Verbatim copying of text, code, data or visual content without quotation marks, citation or any indication of the original author.

Diagnostic characteristics

  • Identical reproduction of sentences or paragraphs
  • No quotation marks
  • Complete absence of citation
  • Usually the most blatant and most easily detected form

As submitted — no marks, no citation

“Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that employs neural networks with many layers. These networks can learn complex patterns in large datasets, enabling breakthroughs in image recognition, natural language processing, and autonomous systems.”

Textbook-level content a student may consider common knowledge — yet the specific wording belongs to its author. Quotation marks plus a citation converts the offence into scholarship.

Overlap ratio = (matched words / total words) × 100%

A directly copied paragraph typically exceeds 85–90% overlap within that segment — which is why a document-level index of 12% can still conceal a wholly copied paragraph.

Type 2 of 4

Self-plagiarism: ownership does not exempt you

Reuse of one's own previously published work, in whole or part, without proper disclosure or citation.

Manifestations in CS research

  • Submitting the same paper to multiple journals — duplicate publication
  • Reusing substantial portions of methodology sections without citation
  • Recycling published datasets, code or algorithms without acknowledgement

Three harms, independent of who wrote it

  • Misrepresentation of novelty — readers believe the work is original
  • Copyright infringement — publishers hold exclusive rights
  • Abuse of peer review — duplicate submission wastes reviewer effort

Illustration

A 2022 paper in Journal A on “Efficient Graph Neural Networks for Social Networks”, substantially reproduced in a 2024 submission to Conference B without citing the earlier work — self-plagiarism, notwithstanding common authorship.

Mitigation

  • Disclose prior related work in the cover letter and the manuscript
  • Quote and cite reused exact text
  • Obtain publisher permission where required
  • Confine overlap to cited methodology and standard references

The legitimate case: a conference paper extended into a journal article is entirely acceptable when the extension is disclosed, the earlier paper cited, and the added contribution stated.

Type 3 of 4

Mosaic plagiarism: the fastest-growing category

Combining text, ideas or data from multiple sources with only superficial modification — synonym substitution, sentence restructuring, light paraphrase — and without proper citation.

Original

“Convolutional neural networks have revolutionized image classification by automatically learning hierarchical features from raw pixel data.”

Mosaic version — still plagiarism

“CNNs have transformed image categorization by autonomously extracting multi-level representations directly from unprocessed pixel inputs.”

Characteristics

  • No single source dominates — hence “mosaic”
  • Modifications cosmetic; no evidence of understanding
  • Citations absent or selectively applied
  • Resists exact-string matching entirely
cos(u, v) = (u · v) / (‖u‖ ‖v‖)

Vectors u and v represent the original and submitted passages. Values above roughly 0.80 signal significant semantic overlap. Near-parallel vectors mean near-identical meaning regardless of surface wording.

The operative test: genuine paraphrase requires comprehension, restructuring of the argument, and citation. Synonym substitution alone is disguise, not scholarship.

Type 4 of 4

Accidental plagiarism: no intent required

Plagiarism occurring without intent to deceive — from ignorance of citation conventions, careless note-taking, or structural imitation.

1 · Forgotten citations

Reproducing information without having recorded the reference.

2 · Improper paraphrasing

Insufficient transformation, with no quotation marks.

3 · Structural replication

Following a source's organisational outline too closely.

4 · Cryptomnesia

Subconscious reproduction of material believed to be original.

5 · Misattribution

Citing a secondary source in place of the original, or the reverse.

Illustration

A student summarises a Wikipedia paragraph, omits the citation because they “thought it was common knowledge”, and submits it. Accidental — and nonetheless plagiarism under most institutional policies.

Structural remedy, not moral resolve

Because the causes are procedural, the fixes are procedural: a literature log, a reference manager, and citation capture at the moment of reading rather than at the moment of writing.

Absence of malicious intent does not absolve the author. Most policies treat accidental plagiarism as a punishable offence, typically with a lesser sanction on a first offence.

Emerging and CS-specific

Six further recognised forms

FormDescription
Idea plagiarismAppropriating concepts or theoretical frameworks without attribution
Code plagiarismCopying source code, algorithms or software components without credit — acutely relevant in CS
Data plagiarismReusing datasets without permission or acknowledgement
Image plagiarismReproducing figures, diagrams or screenshots without citation
Translation plagiarismTranslating a foreign-language source and presenting it as original
AI-assisted plagiarismSubmitting AI-generated text as one's own, or paraphrasing AI output without disclosure

Code is not exempt because reuse is normal

Open-source licences — MIT, GPL, Apache, BSD — impose specific attribution formats. Copying a Stack Overflow snippet or a GitHub function without the required notice is simultaneously an academic and a licensing violation.

AI-assisted work — the live question

Venues differ, but the emerging norm is disclosure of AI assistance plus author responsibility for verification. Unverified AI output additionally risks fabricated citations.

Detection

Five methodological approaches

ApproachMethodologyStrengthsLimitations
String matchingExact or near-exact text comparisonFast, transparentFails on paraphrased content
FingerprintingHash-based indexing of n-gramsEfficient over large corporaSensitive to word-order change
StylometryStatistical analysis of writing styleDetects ghostwritingNeeds substantial reference text
Semantic similarityNLP embedding comparisonIdentifies paraphraseComputationally intensive
Citation analysisExamination of citation patternsDetects missing citationsLimited to structured documents

Method maps to offence type

String matching catches direct plagiarism · fingerprinting scales it to web-sized corpora · semantic similarity catches mosaic plagiarism · stylometry addresses authorship, not overlap.

Code needs its own algorithms

Identifier renaming, statement reordering and reformatting defeat text comparison entirely — which is why token-based and structural methods exist for source code.

Capture as you read

  • Maintain a structured literature log with quotations and references
  • Use Zotero, Mendeley or EndNote so citation data is never reconstructed from memory

Write responsibly

  • Know the style your venue mandates — IEEE and ACM for computing
  • Comprehend, restructure the argument, then cite
  • Use quotation marks for verbatim text beyond standard terminology

Attribute fully

  • Attribute ideas, not only words — a paraphrased concept still needs a citation
  • Disclose prior work when extending your own publications
  • Honour open-source licence attribution formats

Verify before submitting

  • Use AI tools responsibly — disclose where required, verify all output
  • Run pre-submission checks through institutional Turnitin or iThenticate
Capture the citation while reading, not while writing. Nine of the ten safeguards fail silently if deferred to the drafting stage.

Practical application

Matching instrument to purpose

Text and manuscript

Turnitin — institutional student submissions; web, student-paper repositories and publications; similarity index with highlighted matches; LMS integration. Weak on well-paraphrased text and ideas.

iThenticate — publisher-side, built on the CrossCheck database, integrated with editorial workflow. Subscription; primarily textual.

Grammarly · Copyscape · PlagScan · Urkund/Ouriginal — lightweight, web-oriented or institutional; smaller databases.

Source code — essential in CS

MOSS — Measure of Software Similarity: purpose-built for code, using winnowing and fingerprinting adapted to programming structure. The standard for programming assignments.

JPlag — open-source, multi-language, token-based comparison.

AI-text detectors

GPTZero · Originality.ai · Turnitin AI Writing Detection · Copyleaks — estimate machine generation from perplexity, burstiness and stylistic pattern.

Critical caveat. AI-detector reliability remains contested, with documented false-positive rates — non-native English writers and heavily edited prose are disproportionately flagged. Such output is a prompt for human inquiry, never evidence of misconduct on its own.

Practical application

The index is a pointer, not a verdict

< 10%Generally acceptable
10–20%Review required
20–40%Significant overlap; likely revision
> 40%High concern; restructure or cite properly

Thresholds are institution- and journal-specific. IEEE permits up to 30% for review articles under certain conditions; the UGC Regulations 2018 set graded levels for theses with escalating consequences, beginning at a tolerance of up to 10%.

Five interpretation steps

  1. Identify sources — do matches come from cited works?
  2. Recognise quoted material — exclude legitimate quotations
  3. Exclude common knowledge — standard terminology
  4. Allow methodology overlap — standard methods recur legitimately
  5. Verify reference-list exclusion is configured correctly

The two symmetric errors

32% can be clean — if composed of quoted, cited and bibliographic matches.
6% can be serious — if that 6% is an uncited verbatim paragraph.

Case studies

Three cases, and what follows a confirmed violation

Hwang Woo-suk (2004–05). Landmark Science papers claiming stem-cell cloning; investigation revealed fabricated data and duplicated figures, with image reuse and uncredited methodology. Retraction, revoked funding, criminal conviction. Lesson: applying detection to figures and data reveals what textual review misses.

Springer retraction (2019). A CS paper retracted after editors identified substantially overlapping content in two venues without cross-citation. Lesson: self-plagiarism is serious even where the author owns both works.

Code plagiarism in programming courses. MOSS-based studies report 15–25% of submitted assignments containing significant overlap in some cohorts. Mitigations: MOSS or JPlag screening, per-student parameterisation, viva voce verification, integrity education from week one.

Academic

Zero grade · course failure · probation · suspension · expulsion · degree revocation

Professional

Retraction · journal and conference blacklisting · loss of funding · termination

Legal

Copyright infringement suits · breach of publisher agreements · damages and injunctions

Reputational — the most durable

Durable loss of credibility · difficulty securing collaborations · a retraction notice is a permanent, indexed, citable record

Consolidation

What to retain, and what the examiner asks

Five takeaways

  • Plagiarism is uncredited appropriation of ideas, text, data and code
  • Four types: direct, self, mosaic, accidental — differing in characteristic and severity
  • Detection combines fingerprinting, semantic similarity and stylometry
  • Similarity indices demand contextual interpretation, not threshold arithmetic
  • CS raises distinctive challenges in code plagiarism and AI authorship

Exam focus

  1. Be precise about the boundaries between the four types
  2. Memorise the ORI, IEEE and UGC definitions
  3. Explain n-gram fingerprinting and cosine similarity qualitatively
  4. Pair each tool with its use case — MOSS → code, iThenticate → manuscripts
  5. Expect applied case items: classify, then remediate
  6. Cite correctly even within examination answers

Applied scenario

Work this one. A manuscript reuses four paragraphs verbatim from the authors' own 2021 conference paper with neither citation nor quotation marks, and paraphrases two further paragraphs from a 2019 journal article without attribution. Classify each instance and prescribe the corrective action.
