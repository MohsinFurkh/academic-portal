Orientation

Four tiers — distinguished by how each is enforced

TierInstrumentsEnforcement mechanism
1 · International codesNuremberg (1947), Helsinki (1964, rev. 2013), Belmont (1979)Moral authority; no direct enforcement
2 · Professional codesACM Code of Ethics (2018), IEEE Code of EthicsMembership status, venue policy, reputation
3 · National law and guidanceICMR (2017), UGC (2018), DPDP Act (2023), IT Act (2000/2008), CDSCO, MeitYLegally binding; penalties attach
4 · Institutional bodiesIECs / IRBs, Research Integrity Offices, Departmental Research CommitteesOperational gatekeepers — approve, refuse, monitor

IEC

A multidisciplinary body reviewing research involving human participants or sensitive data.

IRB

The US equivalent; used interchangeably in the literature.

Similarity index

Percentage of matched text reported by detection software.

Research misconduct

Fabrication, falsification or plagiarism in proposing, performing, reviewing or reporting research (ORI).

The tiers interlock: a UPES-IEC decision applies Belmont principles through ICMR procedure — so an institutional refusal ultimately rests on a 1979 document. Tier 4 is the only layer you will personally interact with.

The Nuremberg Code

Arising from the trial of Nazi physicians. Ten principles, foremost voluntary informed consent — which now undergirds modern data-protection law and CS user studies alike.

Declaration of Helsinki

World Medical Association. Extends Nuremberg and distinguishes therapeutic from non-therapeutic research. The most widely adopted international standard; referenced by Indian institutional policy.

The Belmont Report

Three principles that now structure IEC review: respect for persons (autonomy, consent), beneficence (minimise harm), justice (fair distribution of burdens and benefits).

Belmont is the examinable one because IEC review forms are effectively operationalised Belmont: consent procedures test respect for persons, risk–benefit analysis tests beneficence, and recruitment criteria test justice.

All three were written for medical research — yet each applies directly to a CS usability study, a crowdsourced annotation task, or an adversarial-testing protocol involving people. The object of protection is the participant, not the discipline.

Tier 2

Professional codes, and the limits of enforceability

ACM Code of Ethics (2018) — general obligations

  • Contribute to society and human well-being
  • Avoid harm
  • Be honest and trustworthy
  • Be fair and take action not to discriminate
  • Respect privacy
  • Honour confidentiality

IEEE Code of Ethics

Commits members to treat all persons fairly and with respect, to refrain from harassment and discrimination, to avoid injuring others, and explicitly requires responsible disclosure of factors that might endanger the public.

Note the structure of both: they impose positive duties — contribute, act against discrimination, disclose danger — and not merely prohibitions. Students read all ethics as a list of prohibitions and conclude that doing nothing is always safe. The ACM code says otherwise.

Enforceability in practice

Sanctioned through membership status, publication venue policy and reputational consequence rather than by law. A code violation rarely reaches a court — but can end a publication record.

Why they matter to computing

ACM's “avoid harm” and anti-discrimination duties are the instruments under which algorithmic bias and unsafe deployment are argued as ethics violations rather than merely engineering defects.

Tier 3

Five bodies, and what each can do to a researcher

ICMR — National Ethical Guidelines (2017)

India's most comprehensive ethical standard and the source of IEC composition and procedure rules. ICMR has additionally issued dedicated ethical guidance on AI in biomedical research and healthcare — algorithmic transparency, dataset representativeness, bias mitigation.

UGC

The (Promotion of Academic Integrity and Prevention of Plagiarism) Regulations, 2018 define plagiarism levels and penalties for higher education institutions. Subsequent draft revisions address similarity thresholds and generative-AI content.

MeitY

National Strategy for AI (2018) and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — the operative Indian data-protection statute, which applies to your research data handling, not only to companies.

CDSCO

Regulates clinical trials and software as a medical device (SaMD) — increasingly relevant where CS departments build AI/ML diagnostic tools.

IT Act, 2000 (am. 2008)

The legal foundation for prosecuting unauthorised data access, digital fraud and cybercrime — the statute under which a research data-handling failure becomes a criminal rather than an academic matter.

Naming the correct instrument is a marking criterion. Confusing UGC (academic integrity) with DPDP (data protection) is a common and costly error.

Institutional bodies · 1 of 3

Who sits on the committee, and why

Four purposes

  1. Protection of participants' rights and welfare
  2. Assurance that risks are minimised and reasonable
  3. Verification that consent procedures are adequate
  4. Oversight through continuing review

Composition

Multidisciplinary and gender-balanced, minimum five and maximum twelve members under ICMR 2017:

  • Chairperson — external to the institution
  • 1–2 basic medical scientists · 1–2 clinicians
  • A legal expert or retired judge
  • A social scientist or philosopher
  • A layperson representing community interests
  • Member Secretary — day-to-day administration

The CS-specific addition

Where proposals involve large-scale personal-data processing, the committee should additionally include a data protection officer or information-security expert — without whom it cannot competently assess re-identification risk.

Why multidisciplinarity is not decoration

The external chair guards against institutional self-interest; the legal member reads consent documents as enforceable instruments; the layperson tests whether disclosure is actually comprehensible to a participant.

Submission carries the proposal, consent forms, data management plan, recruitment materials and risk–benefit analysis. Notification typically follows within 4–6 weeks; “modifications required” is the most common outcome and is not a rejection.

Exemption is a determination the committee makes, not one the investigator may assume. Self-exempting a protocol is itself a procedural violation — and a protocol amended mid-study without resubmission is operating without approval.

Four to six weeks plus a modification cycle means ethics review must be initiated at proposal stage, not when data collection is about to begin.

Institutional bodies · 3 of 3

Does your project need review?

What triggers UPES-IEC review

  • Human participants — surveys, interviews, behavioural experiments, usability studies
  • Personally identifiable information collected from data subjects
  • Animal subjects — rare in CS, required for some HCI work
  • Environmental samples of biological origin
  • Dual-use research of concern (DURC)

Submission and cadence

Proposals route through the online Research Management System (RMS) portal. The committee meets quarterly, with provision for emergency review of time-sensitive studies.

For a CS final-year project

A usability study, a developer survey, a crowdsourced annotation task or an adversarial-testing protocol with human participants all require review. A purely computational study on a public, properly licensed, de-identified dataset generally does not.

DURC is listed deliberately. Research with legitimate civilian use that is also weaponisable — vulnerability-discovery tooling, disinformation-capable generative models, surveillance automation — is within the committee's remit even with no human participants at all.

Policy

UGC 2018: definition, tiers and revisions

UGC definition: “the practice of taking someone else's work or ideas and passing them off as one's own” — encompassing verbatim copying, close paraphrasing without acknowledgement, uncited use of methodologies, datasets or algorithms, self-plagiarism and collusion.
Up to 10%Minor — warning; no penalty if properly cited
10–40%Moderate — denial of degree / withdrawal of paper
Above 40%Major — expulsion; blacklisting of authors

Exclusions: quotes, bibliography, small matches. Thresholds are floors for penalty — an institution may be stricter, not laxer.

Proposed revisions under discussion

  • Exclusion of generative-AI output from similarity detection
  • Mandatory declaration of AI-assisted content generation
  • Stricter thresholds for doctoral theses
  • Explicit treatment of code plagiarism using MOSS or JPlag

Note the third clause of the definition: methodologies, datasets or algorithms. A borrowed pipeline or dataset is regulated content, not just borrowed prose.

Verify the tiers and penalties against the gazette text before relying on exact figures in an examination answer.

Core

Seven contested areas — each tagged to a code

1 · Consent in online studies

Participants may not read forms · deception is sometimes necessary (phishing susceptibility) · withdrawal after anonymisation is technically difficult.

2 · Privacy and anonymisation

Social media, mobile and IoT datasets remain re-identifiable; hence differential privacy, k-anonymity, federated learning.

3 · Algorithmic bias

Hiring tools disadvantaging women (Amazon, 2018) · facial recognition with elevated error rates for women and people of colour · predictive policing. ACM: non-discrimination.

4 · Dual-use (DURC)

Generative models capable of disinformation · vulnerability-discovery tooling · surveillance automation. IEEE: public-endangerment disclosure.

5 · Authorship disputes

Omitted contributors, honorary authorship, competing priority claims.

6 · Whistleblowing

Loyalty to colleagues against responsibility to the public. Institutional protection aligned to the Public Interest Disclosure and Protection of Informers Act, 2010.

7 · Reproducibility and fabrication

Publication pressure driving selective reporting, p-hacking and, at the extreme, invented results.

Ethics is not simply prohibition: some valid questions cannot be studied with full prior disclosure, which is why a committee exists — to weigh justification, debriefing and harm rather than apply a blanket rule.

Practical application

Thresholds, workflow, consequences — and a worked case

Tooling

Turnitin for textual submissions · Ouriginal for cross-checking · MOSS for code at the supervising faculty's discretion.

Thresholds, excluding bibliography and quotes

M.Tech / PhD dissertations ≤ 10%
B.Tech project reports ≤ 20%
Conference / journal publications ≤ 15% (excluding self-citation)

Escalating consequences

First offence — written warning and mandatory resubmission
Second — grade “F” and six-month debarment
Third, or major plagiarism above 40% — expulsion, and degree revocation if discovered post-graduation

DRC

Generative-AI policy

Generative AI is permitted for grammar and code suggestion, subject to explicit declaration in the Acknowledgement section. Submitting AI-generated text as one's own original writing constitutes academic misconduct.

Worked case

Aditi's B.Tech report. Initial index 22%, of which 14% is a methodology section closely paraphrasing a published survey without quotation marks. Threshold is 20%, so it fails. She rewrites, cites the survey and quotes technical definitions; the revised report returns 9% and the DRC accepts it.
Had she not revised: grade “F”, and the supervisor must report to the Departmental Disciplinary Committee.

Consolidation

Five takeaways, and five questions

Five takeaways

  • Enforcement runs through a four-tier architecture — international, professional, national, institutional
  • IECs are the operational pivot; UPES-IEC applies ICMR standards
  • Plagiarism policy works through thresholds and tiered consequences; generative AI is the live area
  • CS dilemmas — online consent, re-identification, bias, dual use, authorship, reproducibility
  • Cases show violations are typically foreseeable; independent review is the missing element

Question 1 · exam format

Analyse the Facebook contagion study using the Belmont framework — which principles were violated, and how might an IEC review have altered the design?

Question 2

Compare the ACM and IEEE codes. To what extent are they enforceable in practice, and what does that imply?

Question 3 · exam format

Examine Cambridge Analytica under the DPDP Act, 2023 — what liabilities would researchers face today at an Indian institution?

Questions 4 and 5

Can consent be meaningfully obtained for large pre-existing datasets? · Where should the line fall on dual-use research?

Across eight lectures this unit moved from why literature matters, through how to analyse it, to the obligations that make the resulting knowledge trustworthy — and this lecture supplies the machinery that enforces them.

Exam preparation

Numbers, documents, distinctions, technique

What is tested

  • The numbers: UGC and UPES thresholds with penalty tiers · IEC size (5–12) · notification window (4–6 weeks) · UPES thresholds by degree type
  • The documents: Nuremberg (1947) · Helsinki (1964, rev. 2013) · Belmont (1979) and its three principles · ICMR (2017) · UGC (2018) · DPDP Act (2023) · ACM Code (2018) · IT Act (2000/2008)
  • The distinctions: exempt vs expedited vs full board · Nuremberg vs Helsinki · plagiarism vs self-plagiarism vs collusion · IEC vs IRB (jurisdictional, not substantive)

Four-move case analysis

  1. Identify the ethical issue
  2. Name the governing code or regulation
  3. State what review or disclosure was owed
  4. Recommend the remedy

Tay → ACM “avoid harm” · Cambridge Analytica → DPDP / GDPR · the thesis case → UGC 2018. Always link a dilemma to a specific instrument.

Expect a current-affairs slant on generative AI and DPDP Act enforcement, and be able to explain why IEC multidisciplinary composition matters rather than merely listing its members.

The practical test of this unit: before your next submission, can you name the committee, the threshold and the declaration that apply to it?
