The core idea

Analysis examines the bricks. Synthesis builds the wall.

Analysis · one paper at a time

Take each paper apart

What did it do, how, on which data, with what result, and what are its weaknesses?

“U-Net uses skip connections and was tested on 2D microscopy images.”

Synthesis · across papers

Weave the papers together

Where do they agree, disagree or build on each other, and what does none of them address?

“U-Net variants dominate BraTS, but all assume four clean MRI sequences.”

The research gap is the hole left in the wall.

A · Critical reading

Read every paper in up to three passes

Pass 1

5–10 minutes

The bird's-eye view

Title, abstract, introduction, headings, conclusion; glance at figures.

Decide: is it relevant? Most papers stop here.

Pass 2

About an hour

The content

Study figures, tables and the method. Note data, metrics and baselines; skip proofs.

Output: one row in your extraction matrix.

Pass 3

Several hours

The deep dive

Mentally re-implement it. Question every assumption and choice.

Only for the 5–10 papers your work builds on.

Based on S. Keshav, “How to Read a Paper,” ACM SIGCOMM Computer Communication Review, 2007

A · Critical reading

Six questions for any paper

1 · Data

Which dataset, which MRI sequences, how many patients? Is it like mine?

2 · Baselines

Compared against strong recent methods, or weak old ones?

3 · Metrics

Dice and Hausdorff distance per sub-region, or only pixel accuracy?

4 · Generalisation

Tested on scans from other hospitals and scanners?

5 · Reproducibility

Code, trained weights and data splits released?

6 · Limitations

What did the authors admit, and what did they leave out?

A · Critical reading · why metric 3 matters

Suppose a tumour fills 1% of the voxels in a scan.
A model that predicts “no tumour” everywhere is 99% accurate, and useless.

Dice score

Dice = 2|A ∩ B| / (|A| + |B|)

A = predicted tumour voxels · B = true tumour voxels

Dice measures overlap with the tumour itself, so the empty prediction scores 0. Papers that report only accuracy deserve suspicion.

B · The critical insight

A benchmark scan is not a hospital scan

BraTS benchmarkRoutine scan in a district hospital
All four MRI sequences (T1, T1ce, T2, FLAIR)Often one or two sequences missing
Skull-stripped, co-registered, resampledRaw scans at varying resolutions
Expert labels agreed by protocolNo labels at all
Scanners from contributing research centresDifferent scanners and protocols
Evaluated offline on large GPUsModest hardware, results needed quickly
A high Dice score on BraTS does not guarantee a high Dice score in a district hospital.

C · Synthesis

A summary lists papers. A synthesis makes an argument.

✗ Summary

Ronneberger et al. proposed U-Net for biomedical segmentation. Isensee et al. proposed nnU-Net, which configures itself. Hatamizadeh et al. proposed Swin UNETR using transformers.

Three separate facts. No link, no point.

✓ Synthesis

Since U-Net [1], encoder–decoder networks have dominated brain tumour segmentation, and both self-configuring pipelines [3] and transformer hybrids [4] now perform strongly on BraTS. However, these results assume curated inputs with all four MRI sequences and substantial GPU resources. How such models perform on incomplete, heterogeneous clinical scans with modest hardware remains insufficiently studied.

Connect the papers → contrast benchmark with reality → end on the gap

C · Synthesis

Three ways to organise the same papers

Chronological

By time: how the field evolved.

U-Net → 3D U-Net → nnU-Net → Swin UNETR → MedSAM

Best for: background sections

Thematic

By question: what the field keeps asking.

Architectures · loss functions · missing sequences · generalisation

Best for: most literature reviews

Methodological

By how studies were done.

CNN vs. transformer vs. foundation model; 2D vs. 3D; benchmark vs. clinical validation

Best for: justifying your method

C · Thematic synthesis

Group your papers by the question they answer

Architectures

Many papers

U-Net family vs. transformer hybrids. They agree skip connections matter; they debate whether transformers justify their cost.

Loss functions

Many papers

Shared problem: tiny tumours, huge background. Dice loss (V-Net) and Dice + cross-entropy are the common answers.

Missing sequences

Few papers

Hetero-modal methods such as HeMIS (Havaei et al., 2016) segment with whichever MRI sequences are available.

Generalisation

Few papers

Scanner and protocol differences are widely recognised, yet few of her papers test on data outside BraTS.

Crowded themes show where the field agrees. Thin themes point to gaps.

D · Research gaps

Five kinds of gap to look for

Gap typeQuestion to askYour version
ContextTested in this setting?Routine scans from Indian hospitals, not only BraTS
DataIs there suitable data?Few public, labelled sets with realistic missing sequences
MethodDoes the method handle this condition?Modern 3D models assume all four sequences
EvaluationAre the right things measured?Dice reported; inference time on modest GPUs rarely is
PopulationDoes it work equally for all groups?Performance across tumour grades and age groups seldom broken down
Pick one or two. A gap that tries to fix everything is really a scope problem.
CSEG3060 · Unit III · Part 2 — Literature Analysis & Synthesis15

D · Research gaps

From gap to gap statement

Template

“Although [prior work] has achieved [result] under [conditions], [specific limitation] remains unaddressed, particularly for [your context]. This study addresses it by [your approach].”

Your gap statement

“Although 3D encoder–decoder and transformer models perform strongly on the BraTS benchmark, they assume all four MRI sequences are available, a condition often unmet in routine clinical practice. This study evaluates and adapts a lightweight 3D segmentation model that remains accurate when one or more sequences are missing, tested on scans from Indian hospitals.”

Key takeaways

From papers to a problem worth solving

1

Read in passes; save the deep read for the papers you build on.

2

Judge each paper against your conditions, not only its own.

3

Synthesis connects, contrasts and ends on what is missing.

4

A real gap is traced to the literature and confirmed by a targeted search.
