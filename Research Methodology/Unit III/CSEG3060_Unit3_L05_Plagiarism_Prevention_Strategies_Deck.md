## Slide 3 — From Philosophy to Practice: The Four Operational Pillars

**Slide Content**
- **Where this lecture sits:** earlier lectures established that research is a **cumulative enterprise** and that misrepresenting the source of an idea is among the gravest integrity violations. Knowing this is *necessary but not sufficient*.
- **Four interrelated competencies:** **paraphrasing** — restating ideas in one's own voice while preserving meaning · **citation styles** — the formal language that credits sources unambiguously · **reference management** — systematic organisation of bibliographic data · **detection tools** — the technological verification layer (Turnitin, URKUND) that confirms originality.
- **Working vocabulary:** *paraphrase* — restatement in different words **and structure**, meaning preserved · *citation* — in-text acknowledgement of a source · *reference list* — the complete set of cited sources · *similarity index* — percentage of submitted text matching comparison databases · *originality report* — the annotated output indicating matched sources and overlap statistics.
- **They are sequential in use and mutually reinforcing:** annotation during reading feeds paraphrasing; the reference manager supplies the citation; the similarity check verifies the result.
- **Assessment framing:** together these constitute the **operational layer of academic integrity** and are evaluated directly under **CO3**.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Establish the pillar ordering as the spine of the lecture so students can see where each subsequent slide fits. Stress the reinforcement point concretely — a well-annotated PDF library is what makes genuine paraphrase possible, because students who paraphrase from a highlighted passage they understood produce different prose than those working from a paragraph they are staring at. Note that the similarity check is last for a reason: it is verification, not a substitute for the first three pillars.

## Slide 4 — What We Are Preventing: A Precise Target

**Slide Content**
- **Policy definition** (UPES Academic Integrity Code; UGC Guidelines 2018): the appropriation of another person's **ideas, processes, results or words** without giving proper credit.
- **Five classified types and their severity:**

  | Type | Description | Severity |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Direct** | Verbatim copying without quotation marks or citation | Most severe |
  | **Mosaic / patchwork** | Mixing phrases from multiple sources without citation | Severe |
  | **Idea** | Using another's conceptual framework without attribution | Severe |
  | **Self** | Reusing one's own published work without disclosure | Moderate to severe |
  | **Accidental** | Unintentional misuse of source material through poor note-taking | Moderate |

- **Why CS is distinctive:** papers routinely contain **algorithms, pseudocode and architectural diagrams**. Copying an algorithm description from a paper and presenting it as one's own contribution is plagiarism **even if the words are modified** — the intellectual contribution, the *idea* of the algorithm, belongs to its author.
- **The prevention mapping:** accidental plagiarism is defeated by **reference management**; mosaic plagiarism by **genuine paraphrasing**; direct plagiarism by **quotation and citation**; idea plagiarism by **attributing concepts, not only sentences**.
- **Consequence for this lecture:** each pillar targets a specific failure mode — the techniques are not interchangeable good habits.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Keep this slide brisk since Lecture 4 covered the typology in depth; its purpose here is to establish the target so each technique can be justified against a specific offence. Dwell only on the CS-distinctive point, because students consistently believe that rewriting an algorithm description in their own words discharges the obligation, when the idea itself still requires citation. Present the prevention mapping as the organising logic of the remaining slides.

## Slide 5 — Paraphrasing I: Patchwriting Versus Genuine Paraphrase

**Slide Content**
- **The misconception:** that paraphrasing means replacing a few words with synonyms while leaving the sentence structure intact. This practice — **patchwriting** — is a form of plagiarism, because it preserves the original author's **syntax and voice** without attribution.
- **Four requirements of a genuine paraphrase:** the **sentence structure changes completely** · the **vocabulary** suits the writer's own discipline and audience · **fidelity to the original meaning** is maintained without distortion · a **citation** is included even though no original words remain.
- **The five-step method** (Purdue OWL; University of Wisconsin Writing Center): **1 Read and comprehend** the passage thoroughly · **2 Set aside** the original so it cannot be visually copied · **3 Restate** the idea in your own words and structures · **4 Compare** against the original for accuracy and originality · **5 Cite** the source.
- **Why Step 2 is the load-bearing one:** paraphrasing with the source in view produces patchwriting almost automatically, because the eye supplies the original syntax before the mind supplies an alternative.
- **Note the ordering of Steps 3 and 4:** restate first, then verify — checking during composition collapses back into word substitution.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Name patchwriting explicitly and repeatedly, since students who have never heard the term genuinely believe synonym substitution is the skill being asked of them. Make the case for Step 2 as the single highest-value habit: closing the PDF before writing changes the output more than any amount of good intention. Note that Step 4 also guards against the opposite failure — a paraphrase so free that it misrepresents the source, which is a different integrity problem.

Left: a two-column contrast card — "Patchwriting: same structure, new words" versus "Paraphrase: new structure, own vocabulary, citation". Right: the five-step method as a vertical flow with Step 2 ("Set aside the original") highlighted as the pivotal action.

## Slide 6 — Paraphrasing II: A Worked Example

**Slide Content**
- **Original** (Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, 2016, p. 96): *"Deep learning is a particular kind of machine learning that achieves great power and flexibility by learning to represent the world as a nested hierarchy of concepts, with each concept defined in relation to simpler concepts."*
- **Unacceptable — patchwriting:** *"Deep learning is a specific type of machine learning that achieves high power and flexibility by learning to represent the world as a nested hierarchy of concepts, where each concept is defined in relation to simpler ones."* **Critique:** only synonyms are changed — *specific* for *particular*, *high* for *great*, *where* for *with*; the sentence structure is intact, and Turnitin will flag it.
- **Acceptable — genuine paraphrase:** *"By modeling reality through layers of increasingly abstract representations — where each higher-level construct is built upon the lower-level ones — deep learning derives its representational capacity from hierarchical feature composition, distinguishing it from classical machine learning paradigms (Goodfellow et al., 2016)."*
- **Why the second version qualifies:** the sentence is **structurally inverted** (the mechanism now leads), the vocabulary is **domain-appropriate** (*representational capacity*, *hierarchical feature composition*), the **meaning is preserved**, and the **citation is present**.
- **The diagnostic question to ask of your own draft:** could a reader reconstruct the original sentence's skeleton from mine? If yes, it is patchwriting.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Display all three versions simultaneously and let students identify which is acceptable before you reveal it — the middle version looks like work, which is exactly why it traps people. Trace the structural inversion aloud: the original leads with the definition and ends with the mechanism, while the paraphrase leads with the mechanism and ends with the contrast, which is what makes it the writer's own sentence. Leave the diagnostic question on screen as the takeaway they can apply to their own draft tonight.

## Slide 7 — Quotation, Paraphrase or Summary: Choosing the Right Instrument

**Slide Content**
- **Comparison of the three techniques:**

  | Technique | When to use | Length | Citation requirement |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Quotation** | The original wording is uniquely authoritative or stylistically essential | Exact words; usually ≤ 40 words | Required **plus quotation marks** |
  | **Paraphrase** | The idea matters but the wording does not | Approximately the length of the original | Required (no quotation marks) |
  | **Summary** | A long passage must be condensed to a brief statement | Substantially shorter than the original | Required (no quotation marks) |

- **Rule of thumb for CS theses:** reserve **direct quotation** for definitions, formal theorems and standards language; use **paraphrase** for algorithm descriptions and prior-work summaries.
- **Why quotation is rare in computing:** technical prose is valued for its content rather than its phrasing, so a quotation signals that the exact formulation carries legal, formal or definitional weight.
- **The common error in both directions:** over-quoting, which substitutes assembly for analysis; and under-quoting, where a standard's normative wording is paraphrased and its precision lost.
- **All three require citation.** Removing quotation marks does not remove the obligation — it changes which obligation applies.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Make the CS-specific rule of thumb the centre of this slide, since students arriving from school-level writing habits over-quote heavily and a related-work section built from quotations demonstrates no synthesis at all. Explain the standards-language exception concretely: paraphrasing a *shall* clause from an IEEE or RFC document can change its normative meaning, so quote it. Reinforce the closing point, because the belief that "no quotation marks means no citation needed" is the direct cause of mosaic plagiarism.

**Visual / Layout Recommendation**
Three-column comparison table as the dominant visual, with a decision strip beneath: "Is the exact wording load-bearing? → Quote. Is the idea load-bearing? → Paraphrase. Is only the conclusion needed? → Summarise." CS rule of thumb as a bordered case box.

## Slide 8 — Citation Styles I: APA 7th and IEEE in Practice

**Slide Content**
- **A citation style is a standardised format for documenting sources** — and consistent application is a hallmark of academic rigour. In computing the dominant styles are **IEEE Editorial Style**, **APA 7th Edition** and, for ACM venues, the **ACM Reference Format**.
- **APA 7th — in-text, paraphrase:** *"Recent work has demonstrated the efficacy of transformer architectures for low-resource NLP tasks (Devlin et al., 2019)."*
- **APA 7th — in-text, direct quotation with page number, and the reference entry:** *"The model architecture is a multi-layer bidirectional Transformer encoder" (Devlin et al., 2019, p. 4171).* → Devlin, J., Chang, M.-W., Lee, K., & Toutanova, K. (2019). BERT: Pre-training of deep bidirectional transformers for language understanding. *Proceedings of NAACL-HLT*, 4171–4186. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/N19-1423
- **IEEE — in-text and reference entry:** *"Recent work has demonstrated the efficacy of transformer architectures for low-resource NLP tasks [1]."* → [1] J. Devlin, M.-W. Chang, K. Lee, and K. Toutanova, "BERT: Pre-training of deep bidirectional transformers for language understanding," in *Proc. NAACL-HLT*, 2019, pp. 4171–4186, doi: 10.18653/v1/N19-1423.
- **Note what stays constant:** author set, title, venue, year, pages and DOI appear in both — only the **ordering, punctuation and in-text marker** differ. Learning a style is learning a convention, not new content.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Use the same source rendered in both styles so students see that the underlying bibliographic record is identical and the style is purely presentational — this demystifies the task considerably. Point out that the APA quotation carries a page number while the paraphrase does not, a distinction examiners test. Tell students to master IEEE first since it governs their thesis and most CS venues, and to treat APA as a readable second language.

**Visual / Layout Recommendation**

## Slide 9 — Citation Styles II: Element Comparison, Selection and Style Drift

**Slide Content**
- **Element-by-element comparison:**

  | Element | APA 7th | IEEE |
  |---|---|---|
  | In-text marker | (Author, Year) | [Number] |
  | Reference ordering | Alphabetical by author | Order of appearance |
  | Article title case | Sentence case | Lower case except proper nouns |
  | DOI format | https://doi.org/… | doi: 10.… |
  | Author name format | Last, F. M. | F. Last |

- **Which style, when:** **IEEE** — IEEE, ACM, Springer LNCS and Elsevier submissions, and most B.Tech/M.Tech theses in Indian universities including UPES · **APA** — HCI, cognitive science, education research and interdisciplinary work · **ACM Reference Format** — for ACM venues specifically.
- **Always verify against the target venue or supervisor's requirement** before writing; retrofitting a style across a completed thesis is expensive and error-prone.
- **Style drift is a real failure mode:** beginning in APA and switching to IEEE mid-document produces a mixed bibliography that IEEE reviewers reject outright and that even APA-only evaluators flag as inconsistency.
- **The structural remedy:** fix the style at the **start** of the document, configure the reference manager to that style exclusively, and verify the generated bibliography against the official style manual before submission.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Draw attention to the ordering row, because it has a practical consequence students miss — IEEE numbering by order of appearance means inserting a new citation renumbers everything downstream, which is precisely why manual citation management fails at thesis scale. Present style drift as an ordinary consequence of writing a document over several months rather than as carelessness, so the remedy reads as workflow design. This slide sets up the reference-management pillar: the tool is what makes consistency automatic.

**Visual / Layout Recommendation**
Upper half: the five-row element comparison table with the In-text marker and Reference ordering rows emphasised. Lower half: a "style drift" case box showing a mixed bibliography with the inconsistent entries marked in red, and the three-step remedy as a green footer strip.

## Slide 10 — Reference Management I: The Big Three Compared

**Slide Content**
- **Why software is not optional:** a B.Tech thesis typically carries **30–80 references**; at that scale manual citation management guarantees errors in ordering, formatting and completeness.
- **Feature comparison:**

  | Feature | Mendeley | Zotero | EndNote |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Developer | Elsevier | Roy Rosenzweig Center (open source) | Clarivate |
  | Cost | Free, optional premium | Free, open source | Paid institutional licence |
  | PDF storage | Excellent | Excellent (with web plugin) | Good |
  | Word integration | Yes | Yes | Yes |
  | Browser capture | Web Importer | Connector | Web capture |
  | Collaboration | Private groups | Group libraries | Shared libraries |
  | Best for | PDF-heavy workflows | Open-source advocates | Large institutional projects |

- **Practical recommendation for this cohort:** either **Mendeley** or **Zotero** — both are free, both integrate with Word and LaTeX, and the choice matters far less than adopting one *before* the writing begins.
- **What the tool actually eliminates:** mis-ordered numeric citations, inconsistent author formats, missing DOIs, orphaned in-text markers and forgotten references — the mechanical causes of accidental plagiarism.
- **What it does not do:** decide whether a citation is *needed*. That judgement remains the author's.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
State plainly that the tool choice is the least important decision on this slide and that adoption timing is the most important — a reference manager installed after the draft exists recovers almost none of its value. Connect back to slide 4's mapping: this pillar is the structural defence against accidental plagiarism, because it removes the human memory step entirely. Ask for a show of hands on who already uses one, and set installation as this week's non-negotiable task.

## Slide 11 — Reference Management II: The Six-Step Workflow and LaTeX Integration

**Slide Content**
- **Six-step workflow:** **1 Capture** — install the browser connector (Mendeley Web Importer, Zotero Connector) and save references directly from Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, SpringerLink and arXiv · **2 Organise** — folders by chapter or topic; rename PDFs as `Author_Year_Keyword_Title.pdf` · **3 Annotate** — highlight and note inside the PDF within the manager, **building the conceptual scaffolding for paraphrasing** · **4 Cite while writing** — insert from the Word or LaTeX plugin; **never type citations manually** · **5 Verify** — cross-check the generated bibliography against the official style manual, since tools miss publication-type nuances such as the conference-versus-journal distinction · **6 Back up** — synchronise to the cloud weekly; a lost library is a research catastrophe.
- **Step 3 is where prevention actually happens:** annotation converts reading into comprehension, and comprehension is the precondition of genuine paraphrase from Slide 5.
- **LaTeX workflow (standard for UPES CS theses):** Mendeley and Zotero export `.bib` files consumed by **BibTeX** (legacy, universally supported) or **biblatex** (modern, more flexible); the **Better BibTeX** Zotero plugin generates stable citation keys such as `vaswani2017attention`.
- **A typical BibTeX record:** `@inproceedings{devlin2019bert, author = {Jacob Devlin and Ming-Wei Chang and Kenton Lee and Kristina Toutanova}, title = {{BERT}: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding}, booktitle = {Proceedings of NAACL-HLT}, year = {2019}, pages = {4171--4186}, doi = {10.18653/v1/N19-1423}}`
- **Step 5 is not optional:** auto-generated entries are a draft, not an authority — verify type, venue abbreviation and page ranges before submission.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Emphasise Step 4 as an absolute rule — manually typed citations are where numbering breaks and where entries silently diverge from the bibliography. Explain the double braces around `{BERT}` in the BibTeX record, since BibTeX will otherwise lowercase the acronym under IEEE style, a small detail that teaches students the tool is a program rather than a formatter. Note Step 6 with a real example of a lost library if you have one; the lesson is remembered far better as anecdote than as instruction.

## Slide 12 — Detection Tools: What Turnitin and URKUND Actually Measure

**Slide Content**
- **The foundational caveat:** these tools **do not detect plagiarism — they detect textual similarity**. Interpretation is a human responsibility. **A match is not plagiarism; an unattributed match may be.**
- **Three comparison databases:** the **internet archive** (billions of continuously updated pages) · **published content** (journal articles, conference proceedings, e-books) · the **institutional repository** (prior student submissions at the same university).
- **How Turnitin works:** submissions are broken into overlapping text segments; each segment is matched against the databases; a **similarity index** is computed as matched text over total submission length; colour-coded highlights link each match to its suspected source. It is integrated into the UPES LMS ecosystem via Moodle/Canvas.
- **URKUND / Ouriginal — distinctive features:** **cross-language detection** identifying translated plagiarism · a **modular API** for LMS integration · **character-by-character analysis**, finer-grained than Turnitin's n-gram approach · a **generative-AI detection module** added in 2023. (URKUND was rebranded Ouriginal after a 2020 merger with PlagScan.)
- **Tool landscape:** **Turnitin** — student work, AI detection since 2023, configurable reference exclusion, available at UPES · **Ouriginal** — academic and research, cross-language · **iThenticate** — research and publication, per-check pricing, manual exclusion · free web checkers — basic reports, no reference exclusion, useful only for self-checking a paragraph.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Lead with the caveat and repeat it, because everything on the next slide depends on students abandoning the belief that the percentage is a verdict. Highlight the institutional-repository database, since it is the reason copying a senior's thesis is the most reliably detected form of misconduct in a university setting. Note the cross-language capability as a genuine methodological advance and the AI-detection modules as contested, so students do not treat either score as conclusive.

## Slide 13 — Reading and Acting on a Similarity Report

**Slide Content**
- **Indicative interpretation bands:** **0–10%** excellent originality, acceptable for most work · **11–24%** acceptable *with proper attribution* — review the highlighted sources · **25–50%** substantial similarity, significant revision required · **above 50%** likely unattributed copying, rewriting and resubmission mandatory. Most Indian universities including **UPES permit a maximum of 10–20% for theses**, excluding references, quotations and common methodological phrasing.
- **Two caveats that cut both ways:** a **0% score does not guarantee originality** — translated, well-paraphrased and non-textual content (code, diagrams) can evade detection; and legitimate matches arise naturally from common phrases, boilerplate and standard methodological description.
- **Five-step action protocol:** **1 Review** each highlighted segment individually · **2 Identify the source** by clicking the match · **3 Classify** each match as properly cited quotation (acceptable) / properly paraphrased with citation (acceptable) / common knowledge or methodological phrase (excludable) / reference-list entry (excludable) / **unattributed overlap (action required)** · **4 Revise** offending passages using the five-step paraphrasing method · **5 Resubmit** and confirm the index falls below the institutional threshold.
- **Worked case — a 27% report.** Composition: 8% properly cited quotations · 12% methodological phrasing common to all CS theses ("the experimental setup was designed to evaluate…") · 5% reference-list titles (excludable) · **2% one uncited paragraph lifted from a survey paper**.
- **Effective similarity index: 2%.** The thesis is in good standing; the single uncited paragraph must be rewritten with attribution. **Always compute the effective index** — tools provide exclusion filters for exactly this purpose.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Make the 27%-to-2% worked case the centre of this slide, because it teaches simultaneously that a scary number can be benign and that a 2% residue can still be a genuine offence requiring action. Walk the classification step slowly, since this is the examinable protocol and the skill assessed by the ten-mark analytical question. Warn students that exclusion settings must be *configured* — a report run without reference exclusion will mislead both them and their supervisor.

## Slide 14 — Case Studies and Your Personal Anti-Plagiarism Workflow

**Slide Content**
- **Case 1 — the patchwriting trap.** *Original (McMahan et al., 2017):* "Federated learning is a machine learning setting where many clients collaboratively train a model under the coordination of a central server, while keeping their training data decentralized." *Student draft:* "Federated learning is a setting of machine learning where many clients train a model collaboratively under the coordination of a central server, while keeping training data decentralized." **Diagnosis:** structure preserved, trivial reordering — patchwriting, and it will be flagged. **Corrected:** "The federated learning paradigm distributes model training across numerous client devices, each retaining local data, while a central coordinator aggregates updates without direct access to the underlying datasets (McMahan et al., 2017)."
- **Case 2 — citation style drift.** A student begins coursework in APA, switches to IEEE for the thesis, and submits a mixed bibliography. **Consequence:** immediate rejection by IEEE reviewers; inconsistency flagged even by APA-only evaluators. **Resolution:** fix the style at the outset, configure the reference manager accordingly, verify against the official manual before submission.
- **The integrated workflow (LO5):** **Read and annotate** in the reference manager → **close the source** → **draft the paraphrase** in your own structure → **insert the citation** from the plugin in the mandated style → **run a pre-submission similarity check** → **compute the effective index** and revise any unattributed overlap → **back up the library**.
- **Each step is traceable to a specific failure mode:** annotation defeats shallow comprehension; closing the source defeats patchwriting; plugin citation defeats numbering and format drift; the similarity check catches what the first four steps missed.
- **Why the order is fixed:** verification is the last step, never the first — a workflow that begins with a similarity check is editing for a score rather than writing with integrity.

**Professor Speaker Notes**
Run Case 1 as a cold-call diagnosis: display the student draft alone, ask whether it is acceptable, and let the class discover that a plausible-looking rewrite is still patchwriting. Present the integrated workflow as the answer to the ten-mark constructive question, and require students to be able to justify each step against the failure mode it defeats rather than merely listing it. Close on the ordering point, since a workflow optimised for a low index rather than for honest writing is the precise inversion this unit exists to prevent.

## Slide 15 — Summary, Discussion Questions & Exam Preparation

**Slide Content**
- **Four takeaways:** **paraphrasing** is a cognitive skill of comprehension, restructuring and citation — never synonym substitution; the five-step method (read, set aside, restate, compare, cite) is the foundation · **citation styles** are formal languages that vary by discipline, IEEE dominating CS, and consistency is non-negotiable · **reference management** eliminates manual citation error and integrates with Word and LaTeX through the six-step workflow (capture, organise, annotate, cite, verify, back up) · **detection tools** produce similarity reports requiring human interpretation, with an effective index below **10–20%** after legitimate exclusions indicating compliance with UPES standards.
- **Preparation actions:** memorise **one** citation style thoroughly — IEEE — and be able to produce both an in-text citation and a reference entry from memory · practise the five-step method on **three** short technical passages · install Mendeley or Zotero and build a library of 5–10 references before the next assignment · self-check a sample paragraph through a free checker before formal submission · read the **UPES Academic Integrity Code** and confirm your department's thesis threshold.
- **Discussion questions:** (i) Why is patchwriting ethically problematic when every word has been changed? (ii) Under what circumstances would you prefer Zotero over Mendeley? (iii) A report returns 32% — what is your step-by-step course of action? (iv) Critically evaluate: *"A high similarity index always indicates plagiarism."*
- **Expected examination items:** the three Turnitin databases · the components of an IEEE in-text citation and reference entry · the UPES maximum similarity index excluding references and quotations · the five-step paraphrasing method with a CS example · Mendeley versus Zotero · the **applied task** — two paraphrased passages with IEEE citations, their reference entries, and a justification of the paraphrasing choices · the **constructive task** — design and justify a personal anti-plagiarism workflow.
- **Suggested reading:** APA *Publication Manual* 7th ed. (2020) · *IEEE Editorial Style Manual* (2022) · Roig (2015), ORI guide to ethical writing · University of Wisconsin–Madison Writing Center, *Paraphrasing* (2023) · UPES *Academic Integrity and Plagiarism Policy* (2023).
