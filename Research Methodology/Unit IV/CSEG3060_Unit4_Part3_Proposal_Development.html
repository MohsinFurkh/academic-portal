Purpose

A proposal does four jobs at once

1 · It persuades

It argues that a specific question is worth answering and that answering it is possible — with the resources asked for, by these people, in this time.

2 · It plans

Writing it forces you to decide the method, the data, the evaluation and the order of work before spending a year discovering them.

3 · It is a contract

Once approved, it is what you will be held to: progress reports, utilisation certificates and the final report are all judged against it.

4 · It is permission

Ethics approval, hospital access, data-sharing agreements and supervisor sign-off are all granted on the basis of this document.

A proposal is your future-work section turned into a plan with a cost. The research in it does not exist yet — which is why every claim must be about what you will do, not what you hope to find.

Purpose

Every reviewer reads with the same three questions

Is it worth doing?

Is the problem real, is the gap real, and would the answer change anything?

Answered by: introduction, literature review, gap statement, expected outcomes

Can it be done?

Is the method sound, is the data obtainable, does the timeline survive contact with reality?

Answered by: methodology, work plan, Gantt chart, risk section, ethics status

Can you do it?

Do you have the skills, the access, the supervision and the facilities?

Answered by: preliminary results, team profile, facilities, letters of access

Preliminary results are the strongest answer to all three. A single figure showing that Dice really does fall from 0.87 to 0.60 on your own pilot scans proves the problem exists, proves you can obtain and process the data, and proves you can run the evaluation — in one image. Do the pilot before writing the proposal.

Objectives

Every objective must name the experiment that discharges it

IDSpecific objectiveMethod that discharges itDeliverable
O1Quantify the Dice loss of three published models on clinical scans with one missing sequenceRe-evaluation on 180 studies from two hospitals, 5 seedsTable 1 + pilot dataset
O2Measure the recovery obtained by sequence-dropout trainingControlled retraining, identical splits and budgetFigure 2
O3Attribute the residual gap to scanner variation or to the missing sequenceAblation across sites with matched acquisitionFigure 3 + ablation table
O4Report inference cost on hardware available in a district hospitalBenchmark on a 4 GB GPU and on CPUTable 4

REJECTED PHRASING

“To study deep learning methods for segmentation.” · “To explore the effect of data quality.” · “To develop an efficient model.”

No experiment discharges these, so no reviewer can check them and no committee can mark them complete.

USABLE VERBS

quantify · measure · compare · attribute · characterise · benchmark · validate

Each one commits you to a number, which commits you to a method — which is exactly what the committee is buying.

Work plan

Objectives → work packages → tasks → milestones → deliverables

1

Work packages

One per objective, plus one for data and one for dissemination. Each has an owner.

2

Tasks

Two to six weeks each. Anything longer is hiding uncertainty; anything shorter belongs in a to-do list.

3

Milestones

A verifiable event with a date: “ethics approval received”, “baseline reproduced within 1 point”.

4

Deliverables

A thing a reviewer can open: a dataset, a table, a report, a repository.

Dependencies decide the order

Ethics approval blocks data collection; data collection blocks training; training blocks the ablation. Anything on this chain is on the critical path, and a week lost there is a week lost overall — while a week lost off the path costs nothing.

Name your critical path explicitly in the proposal. It tells the committee you have thought in dependencies, not in wishes.

The rule that saves student projects

Writing is a task, not an afterthought. Allocate the last 15–20% of the schedule to writing, and put an internal “first complete draft” milestone four weeks before the deadline.

Every project that ran to the last night skipped this one line.

CSEG3060 · Unit IV · Part 3 — Proposal Development8

Timeline

Five ways a Gantt chart tells the committee you have not thought it through

1 · No buffer

Every bar ends exactly where the next begins. The first delay destroys the plan. Reserve 10–15%.

2 · Everything in parallel

Six bars running at once, with one student. Parallel bars must be justified by people.

3 · Approvals not shown

Ethics review and data agreements take months and block everything. If they are not on the chart, the chart is fiction.

4 · Writing at the end only

A single “report writing” bar in the last month. The draft milestone must sit before the deadline.

5 · No milestones

Bars but no verifiable events, so nobody — including you — can tell whether the project is on track in month 5.

The estimation rule: take your honest estimate for anything you have never done before and multiply by two. Reviewers who have run projects do this arithmetic in their heads while reading your chart.

Budget

The standard heads of expenditure

HeadWhat goes in itWhat reviewers check
ManpowerFellowships for project staff — JRF, SRF, project assistant — at the agency's prescribed ratesIs the person needed full-time, and for how long? Usually the largest head.
EquipmentNon-consumable items: a GPU workstation, storage, an instrumentWhy can existing institutional facilities not be used? Quotations attached?
ConsumablesItems used up: storage media, cloud compute credits, licences, reagentsAre the quantities tied to the work plan?
TravelField visits to sites, conference presentation of resultsIs travel tied to a task and a deliverable?
ContingencyA small margin for unforeseen costs, typically a few per centIs it a modest fraction, or a hiding place?
OverheadsInstitutional charge, set by the agency as a percentage of the direct costComputed at the prescribed rate — not invented
Rates, ceilings and permitted heads are set by each agency's current guidelines. Always budget from the live call document, never from an older proposal you were given.

Justification: one sentence per line

A number without a sentence is a request. A number with a sentence is an argument.

Budget

What makes a reviewer distrust a budget

Round numbers everywhere

Every line ending in three zeros means nothing was costed. Attach quotations for equipment.

Equipment that is really a laptop

General-purpose machines the institution should provide. Ask only for what the work plan cannot proceed without.

Budget and work plan disagree

Travel to three sites, but the methodology names two. This is the mismatch reviewers look for first.

Contingency as a slush fund

A large unexplained contingency reads as “we have not planned this”.

Cloud compute with no arithmetic

State GPU-hours × runs × rate. A compute budget that cannot be recomputed is not a budget.

No cost for the boring things

Annotation time, storage, backups and publication charges are real costs that students routinely forget.

The budget is read as evidence about your planning, not as a price list. That is why an honest, small, well-argued budget beats a large vague one.

CriterionThe question behind it
RelevanceDoes it fit the call, the theme, the agency's mandate?
NoveltyIs the gap real, and is it demonstrated from the literature?
SoundnessWill this method actually answer this question?
FeasibilityTime, data, skills, access, equipment — all present?
Team & facilitiesCan these people, in this place, do it?
Outcomes & impactWhat exists at the end, and who uses it?
BudgetIs every rupee tied to a task?
Ethics & complianceApprovals, data protection, declarations

What the committee is really doing

Members are not all specialists in your subfield. One person may present your proposal to the rest from notes. Write so that a competent computer scientist who has never read a segmentation paper can state your gap and your plan after ten minutes.

They are comparing your proposal against others in the same session — so the discriminator is rarely the idea. It is how completely the risks are addressed.

Write to be defended by someone else. A clear objectives table, a real Gantt chart and a justified budget give your advocate on the committee something to point at.

Review committee

The rejections that have nothing to do with the idea

No gap, only a topic

“Deep learning for X” with a literature review that lists papers instead of ending in a missing piece.

Objectives unmeasurable

Nothing the committee could later mark as done.

Method does not answer the question

An explanatory question with a purely descriptive plan, or a claim about clinics tested only on a benchmark.

Timeline impossible

Twelve months of work in six, with ethics approval not shown at all.

No access to the data

Hospital data assumed, with no agreement, no letter and no IEC application in progress.

Budget–plan mismatch

Money for things the plan never mentions, or a plan requiring resources the budget never asks for.

Format violations

Over the page limit, missing sections, wrong template, missing signatures. Many proposals are rejected before review.

Unreadable

Dense, unedited, jargon-heavy prose. Part 1 of this unit exists for exactly this line.

Only two of these eight are about the science. Six are about planning and writing — which is why this unit sits where it does in the course.

Formats

Where proposals go, and the format each one demands

SERB / DST

National competitive schemes for individual investigators and teams. Online portal, prescribed section order, endorsement from the institution, budget heads fixed by the scheme.

AICTE & similar

Schemes aimed at technical institutions: infrastructure, curriculum and applied projects, often with a defined maximum grant.

Institutional seed grants

Your own university's internal calls. Smaller, faster and the realistic first target for a student-led project.

Academic proposals

Your synopsis, dissertation proposal or Assignment 2: the same components, shorter, with the same expectations about objectives, plan and risks.

Read the call document as a specification

Page limits, font size, section order, annexure list, eligibility, deadline and submission mode are hard constraints. Build a checklist from the call and tick it the day before you submit — after the deadline, nobody reads a proposal that breaks them.

Referencing

IEEE numbered style for engineering and computing; ACM where the venue or call specifies it; APA in education and social-science-facing calls. Use a reference manager, as Unit III Part 4 set out, and let the template format the list.

Try it · 25 minutes

Draft the spine of your own proposal

In your team (15 min)

  1. Write the gap statement from Unit III Part 2 at the top of the sheet
  2. Write one general and three specific objectives, each with a measurable verb
  3. Build the objective → method → deliverable table (three rows)
  4. Draw a Gantt chart over your real deadline, with approvals, a writing bar, two milestones and a buffer
  5. List your budget heads with one justification sentence each

Swap and review as a committee (10 min)

Score the other team out of 5 on each and write one sentence of evidence for each score.

  • Is the gap real and stated in one sentence?
  • Could every objective be marked done or not done?
  • Does the timeline include approvals, writing and buffer?
  • Is every budget line traceable to a task?
  • What is the biggest risk, and is it addressed anywhere?

This sheet is the skeleton of Assignment 2, the full research proposal in the prescribed format.

Key takeaways

Five rules for a proposal that survives a committee

1

A proposal persuades, plans, binds and permits. Every claim is about what you will do.

2

Reviewers ask three questions: is it worth doing, can it be done, can you do it — and preliminary results answer all three.

3

Each objective names the experiment that discharges it and the deliverable it produces.

4

A credible Gantt chart shows approvals, dependencies, writing and buffer; a credible budget has a sentence per line.

5

Most rejections are about planning and writing, not about the idea — and format violations are rejected before review.
