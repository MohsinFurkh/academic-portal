Research Methodology in CS

CSEG3060 · Course Content Delivery Plan · Autumn Semester 2026 (04 Aug – 02 Dec 2026)

Course Objectives

  1. Familiarize students with research problem identification.
  2. Introduce various research methodologies in computing science.
  3. Promote ethical conduct in research.
  4. Enhance technical writing and proposal development skills.
  5. Provide insights into intellectual property and patents.

Course Outcomes

  • CO1: Identify and formulate research problems using defined criteria and characteristics.
  • CO2: Utilize various investigative approaches and methodologies for problem-solving in computing science research.
  • CO3: Demonstrate ethical conduct and awareness of research ethics and plagiarism.
  • CO4: Produce technically sound, well-structured research reports and proposals.
  • CO5: Analyze and apply knowledge of intellectual property rights, particularly patents, in computing science.

Unit-wise Lecture Notes

Complete student notes for revision and examination preparation — reading documents, not slide decks. Definition boxes, exam tips, common-mistake warnings, worked examples and practice questions grouped by mark weight. Further units are added here as they are completed.

Unit I 9 syllabus hours

Introduction to Research Problem

What research is and the eleven-step research process; understanding, sourcing and identifying the research problem; the technique of defining it — experience survey, pilot survey and rephrasing; criteria for a good problem and errors in selection; scope and objectives; hypothesis formulation and testing with a fully worked chi-square example; research design (CRD, RBD, Latin square, factorial); investigative approaches, data collection, analysis, interpretation and instrumentation.

CO1 FINER & SMART Experience vs pilot survey Type I & II errors χ² worked example Latin square
Read Unit I notes
Unit II 13 syllabus hours

Research Methods in Computing Science

The three paradigms of computing; the dialectic of research and the thesis–antithesis–synthesis triad; models of argument including the full Toulmin model and the logical fallacies to avoid; the three proof methods — demonstration, empirical and mathematical; deduction, induction and abduction; theoretical models and formal verification; algorithmic and software engineering approaches; mathematical modelling with M/M/1 and Little's Law; performance estimation and evaluation.

CO2 Toulmin model Three proof methods Deduction vs induction M/M/1 & Little's Law Amdahl's Law

Unit III 8 syllabus hours

Ethical Conduct and Literature Studies

Eight lectures, each with a slide deck and a set of student notes: literature studies and their objectives, the four review approaches, literature analysis and gap identification, plagiarism and its prevention, the foundational principles of research ethics, the domains in which they apply, and the regulatory and institutional machinery that enforces them. Notes in preparation

Each set of notes opens as a single reading document with its own contents sidebar, a dark/light toggle and a Print / PDF button that produces a clean printable copy. Notes are written in three layers — definition, plain-language explanation, then numbered points and a computing example — because examiners award marks for points, not paragraphs.
