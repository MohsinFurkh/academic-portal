Administration

Who runs the system, and what has changed

The machinery

  • The Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks heads the office, under the relevant ministry.
  • Four patent offices — Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata — with jurisdiction by the applicant's address.
  • Controllers and examiners decide grant; oppositions go before the Controller with an Opposition Board.
  • Appeals and revocation actions are heard by the appropriate judicial forum, which has changed in recent years — check the current position.

How it has modernised

  • Electronic filing and prosecution, with online status via InPASS
  • Expedited examination for eligible applicants, including start-ups and small entities
  • Reduced fees for natural persons, start-ups, small entities and educational institutions
  • Recruitment of many more examiners, and shorter pendency than a decade ago
  • Awareness programmes and institutional IP cells, and the Traditional Knowledge Digital Library for examiners
The direction of travel is towards faster, cheaper access for small applicants — which is precisely the category a student inventor or a university start-up falls into. Ask your IP cell which concessions you qualify for.

Software

Why software sits awkwardly in the patent system at all

It is already protected

Code is a literary work under copyright — automatic, long, free. Patents were designed for machines and processes, not for text that behaves.

It is close to mathematics

Every program is, at some level, an algorithm — and abstract methods have always been outside patentable subject matter.

It is also genuinely technical

Software controls braking systems, compresses video and manages memory. Refusing all of it would exclude much of modern engineering.

Every jurisdiction has drawn a line somewhere between those three facts, and none of the lines is tidy. India's line is drawn by Section 3(k) and the word “per se”; Europe's by the requirement of technical character; the United States' by the judge-made test for abstract ideas. The practical question for a researcher is the same everywhere: what technical problem does this solve, outside the computer itself?

India

Section 3(k) and the weight carried by two words

The exclusion

Not inventions: a mathematical or business method, a computer programme per se, or algorithms.

The drafting history and later judicial attention have focused on “per se”: the exclusion is read as aimed at the programme as such, rather than at every invention in which a computer appears.

In Ferid Allani, the Delhi High Court directed attention to that phrase and to whether the invention produces a technical effect or technical contribution.

What examiners look for

The Patent Office's guidelines on computer-related inventions steer examination. In substance, the question asked is whether the claimed contribution lies in a technical advance or only in the program logic.

Signals that help: improved performance of the hardware or system itself; a physical process controlled or measured; a resource constraint met; a technical problem stated in the specification and solved by stated means.

Guidelines and case law in this area have been revised more than once. Treat this slide as orientation, not doctrine, and check the current guidelines and decisions before advising anyone.

Practice

The same invention, framed two ways

✗ Framed as a program

A method of segmenting a medical image, comprising: receiving image data; applying a trained neural network to the image data; and outputting a segmentation map.

Why it struggles: the contribution as claimed is the computation itself. Nothing outside the program is improved, and nothing technical is solved — so the objection is that it is a computer programme or algorithm as such, and possibly a method of diagnosis as well.

✓ Framed as a technical solution

An imaging system comprising a memory and a processor configured to: detect which of a plurality of acquisition sequences are present; select a corresponding sub-model such that peak memory use remains below a predetermined budget; and generate a segmentation map without requiring re-acquisition of the missing sequence.

Why it is arguable: it recites a system, identifies a technical constraint (memory budget, incomplete acquisition) and states how the arrangement solves it. Whether it is granted still depends on prior art — but the subject-matter objection now has something to bite on.

The framing must be in the specification at filing. You cannot add a technical-effect story later — new matter is not allowed. This is why Unit V Part 4 insisted on writing it in at month zero.

The other model

Open source: copyright used to guarantee openness

How it works

The author holds copyright and grants a licence with conditions. Break the conditions and you lose the permission — so the licence is enforced by the same copyright it appears to give away.

Permissive vs copyleft

MIT, BSD, Apache: use it almost freely, keep the notices. GPL family: derivative works distributed to others must be released under the same terms.

Patent clauses

Modern licences such as Apache 2.0 include an express patent grant and a termination clause if you sue over patents. Licences are not only about copyright any more.

Three things that catch research groups

1 · Licence compatibility. Pulling a copyleft component into a codebase you intend to license exclusively can make that promise impossible to keep. 2 · Releasing before filing. A public repository is a public disclosure — Unit V Part 1. 3 · Institutional policy. Many universities require approval before open-sourcing work funded under a grant, because the funder's terms may govern.

New developments

Standard-essential patents: when you cannot design around

The mechanism

A standard — a mobile communication standard, a video codec — can only be implemented by practising certain patents. Those are standard-essential patents.

Standards bodies therefore require holders to commit to license on fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory (FRAND) terms, so that adopting the standard does not hand one firm a veto over an entire industry.

Why it is contested

FRAND has no arithmetic definition. Disputes turn on the royalty base — a percentage of the chip or of the whole handset? — on stacking across many holders, and on whether an injunction should be available at all.

Indian courts have heard significant SEP litigation in the telecommunications sector, and the questions are live in many jurisdictions at once.

Why a research student should care: if your work targets a standardised area — codecs, wireless, interoperable medical imaging formats — freedom to operate is not optional, and a licence may be unavoidable however original your contribution is. Check the standard's IPR policy before you build a product on it.

New developments

Artificial intelligence: four questions the system has not settled

Can an AI be an inventor?

Applications naming an AI system as inventor have been refused in most major jurisdictions, which read inventorship as requiring a natural person. The question of how much human contribution suffices remains open.

Who owns AI output?

Copyright generally presumes a human author. Treatment of machine-generated works varies between countries, and the boundary for human-assisted output is unsettled.

Training data

Whether training on copyright works requires permission is being litigated and legislated in several jurisdictions. For your research, the practical constraints are the dataset licence and the ethics approval — Unit III.

Disclosure and obviousness

If a model can generate many variants cheaply, what is “obvious to a person skilled in the art”? And how do you enable an invention whose behaviour depends on weights you cannot fully describe?

Expect all four to change during your career. Do not memorise the answers; learn to locate the current rule — which is the methodological skill this whole course has been teaching.

Try it · 20 minutes

Reframe your own claim for Section 3(k)

In your team (12 min)

  1. Write your Unit V claim as it stands. Mark every element that is pure computation.
  2. Answer in one sentence: what technical problem does this solve outside the program itself? If you cannot, say so honestly — that is a finding.
  3. Rewrite the claim as a system, reciting the technical constraint it satisfies.
  4. Write the two sentences you would add to the specification to support that framing, with a measurement.
  5. Decide the route: patent, publish, open-source, or trade secret — and why.

Swap and object (8 min)

  • Is the “technical effect” real, or just the algorithm restated in hardware words?
  • Is there a measurement behind it, or an assertion?
  • Does the claim now cover so little that a competitor avoids it trivially?
  • Would the claim read on a method of diagnosis performed on a patient?
  • If the answer to question 2 in their list was “nothing outside the program”, is publishing the better route?

“Publish instead” is a perfectly good outcome of this exercise, and the one most student projects should reach.

Key takeaways

Five things about software and the modern IP system

1

The Indian system has moved towards electronic filing, expedited examination and reduced fees for small applicants — including students.

2

Section 3(k) excludes the computer programme per se; attention has centred on technical effect and on those two words.

3

India, Europe and the US use different tests that converge on one question: is the contribution technical?

4

Open source is copyright used to guarantee openness — and releasing code is a public disclosure.

5

SEPs and AI are where the system is currently moving. Learn to find the current rule rather than memorise today's.
